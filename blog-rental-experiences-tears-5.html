Rental Experiences, Tears — The Conclusion

Adeline Quarshie · 30 May 2026 · 9 min read · Part 5 of 5

Closing a Chapter

This is the fifth and final article in a series that began in February 2026. When I started writing, I was not sure how many people would find it useful. What I did not anticipate was how many landlords, investors, and property professionals across Ghana would reach out to say: "This is exactly what happened to me."

That response confirmed what we already suspected — the challenges we faced are not ours alone. They are systemic. And the lessons we learned, the hard way, are ones that every property owner in Ghana needs to absorb before they need them.

By the time we recovered possession of the property in January 2026, our tenant had accumulated five months of unpaid rent. This was a structured financial loss that compounded throughout the dispute, and it was not fully resolved by the institutional processes we engaged. The rent control process, while necessary and available, operates at a pace that does not match the financial pressure a landlord faces in real time.

This is the debt that institutional processes often leave unresolved — the rent owed but never recovered, the security deposit that did not cover the arrears, the legal fees that nobody reimburses. By the time you have "won," you have already absorbed costs that were never in the plan.

"Being legally right is not always enough — you must also be strategically and documentarily right, from the very beginning." Adeline Quarshie, CEO — Credence Real Estate & Asset Management

Lessons Learnt the Hard Way

Real estate rentals are often described as passive income. Our experience has shown that this description can be dangerously misleading. Passive income requires active infrastructure. The "passive" part only works when everything beneath it is structured, documented, and enforced correctly.

What We Would Do Differently From Day One
01
Your Tenancy Agreement Is a Legal Document — Treat It As One

A standard form covers the basics. What it rarely does is specify the precise meaning of key terms: what constitutes a breach, what notice periods mean in practice, and how each clause interacts with the Rent Act. Anything not clearly written may not exist in the eyes of the law.

02
Rent Structure Determines Enforcement Strength

The payment cycle you choose has direct implications for how easily you can enforce arrears. A longer advance period does not only protect you financially — it simplifies your legal position considerably when arrears develop. The structure you set at signing is the structure you will work with in court.

03
Security Deposits Need Firm, Written Boundaries

What can the deposit be used for? When must it be returned? What happens if damages exceed the deposit amount? If your agreement does not answer these questions, they will be answered during a dispute — and not in the way you assumed.

04
Property Use Must Be Precisely Defined

"Residential use only" is not sufficient contractual language. Define which activities are expressly permitted, which are prohibited, and what the immediate consequence is if a tenant converts the use without your written consent. Authority must always be verified, never assumed.

05
Occupancy Limits Require Express Contractual Language

Who is permitted to occupy the property? Can the tenant sublet? What happens if additional occupants move in without approval? These are not hypothetical questions — they become live disputes if they are not answered contractually before occupancy begins.

The Hard Truth About Institutions

Rent Control and the courts are necessary. They exist to provide redress and, when used properly, they provide the framework within which disputes can be resolved. What follows is not a criticism of those institutions — it is an honest account of the realities landlords will encounter when engaging them.

Both operate slowly. In a dispute where rent is accumulating as debt and your property is occupied by a non-paying tenant, "slowly" has a direct financial cost. The legal process that eventually produces the right outcome may do so nine months after the tenancy went wrong. You must plan for that gap — financially and emotionally.

Processes can also be used strategically to create delay. Legal representatives on both sides understand how timelines can be extended. Intimidation, whether subtle or overt, is a feature of some disputes. You must engage your own counsel early, maintain complete documentation from the outset, and resist the temptation to resolve matters informally once the situation has become a formal dispute.

Accountability shifts when formal documentation exists. The presence of written records — notices, correspondence, payment records, inspection reports — changes the dynamic in institutional settings in ways that verbal accounts simply cannot replicate. Document everything, from the first day.

"Authority must always be verified, never assumed. Anything not clearly written may not exist in the eyes of the law." Adeline Quarshie

Above All

Our experience taught us, above everything else, that owning rental property in Ghana demands three things: systems, professional advice, and emotional resilience.

Systems — because everything that goes wrong in a tenancy dispute can be traced back to something that was not documented, not structured, or not enforced correctly. The absence of a system is not a neutral position. It is a risk position.

Professional advice — because the Rent Act, the court process, the enforcement mechanisms, and the rights and obligations of both parties are more complex than any standard tenancy agreement conveys. A property lawyer engaged at the start of a tenancy, rather than in the middle of a dispute, will cost a fraction of what a dispute costs.

Emotional resilience — because disputes test your patience, your confidence, and your willingness to stay the course. The tenant, the lawyers, the institutional timelines — all of it requires you to remain disciplined and clear-headed when the situation is anything but.

Future articles in this space will go deeper into the practical tools available to landlords in Ghana: what a comprehensive tenancy agreement looks like, how to structure rent payments for maximum protection, and how to use formal institutions effectively when disputes arise.

"Real estate rewards preparation and punishes assumption — every single time."
Adeline Quarshie · CEO, Credence Real Estate & Asset Management

If you are a property owner in Ghana — whether you manage your own properties or are considering doing so — I would encourage you not to wait for a dispute to teach you what this series has tried to share.
